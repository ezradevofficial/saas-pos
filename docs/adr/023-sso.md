# ADR 023: Enterprise single sign-on

Status: Accepted (Phase 8, stage 8B: AUTH-11; with AUTH-03, AUTH-09, AUTH-10, RBAC-10, BR-04, BR-05, TEN-01, AUD-01)

## Context

AUTH-11 asks for sign-in with Microsoft Entra ID or Google Workspace, by OIDC or SAML. The concept note makes it a launch requirement. Enterprises expect four things:

- their own identity provider (IdP) per tenant;
- their staff found by email domain on the shared sign-in page, or on the tenant's own host;
- an option to switch password sign-in off for their staff;
- no new way into another tenant, and no way around the app's lockout, sessions and audit.

The owner approved `laravel/socialite`, `socialiteproviders/microsoft` and `onelogin/php-saml` (2026-10-16).

## Decision

### Where it lives

`App\Core\Sso` is a core area, like Identity and Branding. Every module's users sign in through it, so it is not a business module. Three tenant tables carry `tenant_id` under forced row-level security:

- `sso_connections`: one IdP per row.
  - `provider` is `entra`, `google` or `saml`.
  - `settings` (jsonb) holds the public settings: client id, Entra directory id, Google hosted domain, SAML IdP entity id, sign-in URL and certificates.
  - `client_secret` is encrypted with the application key (`encrypted` cast). It is never returned, never exported, and is replaced only by sending a new one.
  - `enabled`, `tested_at`, `last_test` (jsonb) and `archived_at` (TEN-06).
- `sso_domains`: the tenant's email domains, verified by a DNS TXT record (below).
- `sso_identities`: the link between a user and an IdP subject, `(connection_id, subject)` unique.

The tenant's choices are kept in `tenants.settings->sso`. Branding keeps `tenants.settings->branding` the same way. The choices are:

- `enforce`;
- `jit_provisioning`;
- the default role and scope for provisioned users;
- who set that default.

### Who manages it

- **Permission.** Managing SSO takes `core.sso.manage` at tenant scope. The Owner template holds it; the Admin template does not (`except`, as for the full export).
  - Whoever controls the IdP can sign in as any linked user, Owners included. Configuring one is therefore as powerful as being the Owner.
  - An Owner may still put `core.sso.manage` in a custom role. The anti-escalation rules (ADR 006) mean only a holder can grant it.
- **Endpoints.** The configuration endpoints are `GET sso`, `PUT sso/settings`, the `sso/connections` routes and the `sso/domains` routes. Each uses a Form Request that checks the permission.
- **Other tenants.** A connection or domain of another tenant is not found. Route binding runs under RLS.

### Email domains (BR-05 reused, not shared)

The proof is BR-05's, through the same code paths:

- the `DnsResolver`;
- the TXT record name `{branding.domains.txt_prefix}.{domain}`;
- the value `{txt_value_prefix}{token}`;
- the same states: `pending`, `verified` and `failed`.

The claims sit in their own table. A custom domain (`tenant_domains`) is a host the app is served on. It gets TLS, sign-in branding and the email sender. An email domain must get none of these, and serving the app on `company.co.ke` just because staff have addresses there would be wrong.

- **First verifier wins.** Several tenants may hold pending claims on a domain. A partial unique index on verified, unarchived rows lets only the first proof succeed. A later proof fails with `claimed_elsewhere`, as for branding.
- **Re-checks.** `domains:verify` also re-checks verified email domains daily. Three missed records in a row drop a domain to `failed`, and SSO stops for it at once.
  - The fan-out uses `app_tenants_with_sso_domain_checks_due(p_at)` (ADR 002).
  - Failed claims are archived after 7 days.
- **Claims.** A tenant can only claim domains in the form `company.co.ke`. Platform domains (`APP_BASE_DOMAIN` and its subdomains) are refused.

### Finding the tenant before sign-in

Two security-definer functions, listed in ADR 002, return tenant ids only:

- `sso_tenant_for_email_domain(p_domain text)` gives the active tenant with that verified, unarchived email domain. It serves email discovery on the shared sign-in page, and sign-up's check for enforced domains.
- `sso_tenant_for_host(p_slug text, p_host text)` gives the active tenant whose subdomain or verified custom domain the sign-in page is served on (BR-04).

A host is not a credential. It only chooses whose enabled connections are offered. Everything after that runs under the tenant's RLS.

### The flow

1. **Start.** `POST auth/sso/start` takes `email` or `host`, and optionally `connection_id`. With several enabled connections and no `connection_id`, it answers the list (id, name, provider) so the user can choose. Otherwise it creates a flow in the cache (10 minutes) under `sha256(state)`. The flow holds:
   - the tenant and the connection;
   - the nonce and the PKCE verifier (OIDC), or the AuthnRequest id (SAML);
   - the hash of a **browser binding** secret;
   - the return origin;
   - for a test, the tester.

   It answers `redirect_url` and `binding`. The web app keeps `binding` in `sessionStorage` and sends the browser to the IdP.
2. **Callback.**
   - OIDC returns to `GET auth/sso/oidc/callback`, the one redirect URI registered at the IdP.
   - SAML posts to `POST auth/sso/saml/{connection}/acs`.

   The callback validates everything (below) and makes a **ticket** in the cache (2 minutes) carrying the result. It then redirects to `{origin}/sso/complete#ticket=…`. Errors go to `#error=code`. The fragment never reaches a server, a log or a `Referer`.
3. **Complete.** `POST auth/sso/complete` takes the ticket, the binding and the device name, and then:
   - checks the binding against the flow's hash, so a ticket injected into someone else's browser is useless (login CSRF);
   - spends the ticket once;
   - applies the user checks;
   - issues the session through `Authenticate::issueToken`.

**Single use.** States and tickets are spent with `Cache::add` on a "used" key, which is atomic in Redis and in the database store, before they are read. Two callbacks with the same state cannot both pass.

**Return origin.** Only these origins are accepted, so there is no open redirect:

- `app.frontend_url`;
- `https://{slug}.{APP_BASE_DOMAIN}` of the flow's tenant;
- `https://{verified custom domain}` of the flow's tenant.

Anything else falls back to `app.frontend_url`.

### OIDC (Entra ID, Google Workspace)

**Socialite's role.** Socialite's Google provider and the SocialiteProviders Microsoft provider are subclassed (`Sso\Oidc\EntraDriver`, `GoogleDriver`). They build the authorisation request and exchange the code. Our subclasses do three things on top:

- take the endpoints from the IdP's discovery document;
- add `nonce`, `state` and the PKCE `code_challenge` (S256) to the request, and `code_verifier` to the exchange, all from the flow and never from a session;
- never call `user()`, because Socialite reads Graph or userinfo and does not check the ID token.

**ID token checks.** `IdTokenValidator` checks the ID token itself with `firebase/php-jwt`. Socialite already requires that library, so it is a transitive dependency and not a new one.

- **Signature.** Only RS256 is accepted, against the IdP's JWKS, found through the discovery document. Discovery and JWKS are cached for an hour, and the JWKS is fetched again once for an unknown `kid`.
- **Issuer.**
  - For Entra it is exactly `https://login.microsoftonline.com/{directory id}/v2.0`. A connection must name its directory (a GUID): `common`, `organizations` and `consumers` are refused. Pinning the directory closes the multi-tenant "nOAuth" class of attacks.
  - For Google it is `https://accounts.google.com`.
- **Audience** is the client id. With several audiences, `azp` must be the client id.
- **Expiry and issue time** are checked with 60 seconds of leeway.
- **Nonce** must equal the flow's.
- **State.** It is checked before anything else: it is the cache key, and it is spent once.
- **Email.**
  - Google requires `email_verified` true and an `hd` claim (Workspace accounts only).
    - `hd` must be the email's own domain or one of the tenant's verified email domains, so another organisation's Workspace cannot assert our addresses.
    - When the connection names a hosted domain, `hd` must equal it, and that domain must be verified when the connection is saved.
  - Entra's `email` claim is whatever the directory holds, and an Entra admin can type any address there. It is used only when the optional claim `xms_edov` (email domain owner verified) is true. Otherwise the UPN (`preferred_username`) is used, never a bare `email`. The admin registering the app should add `xms_edov` as an optional claim. The address's domain must still be one of the tenant's verified domains, and the directory is pinned.
- **Subject** is `sub`.

The IdP base URLs come from configuration (`sso.entra.authority`, `sso.google.issuer`). Production uses the real ones. Local development and the Playwright check point them at a fake IdP. These are operator settings, never tenant data.

### SAML 2.0

`onelogin/php-saml` checks the response in strict mode. We construct `OneLogin\Saml2\Response` ourselves, so the library never reads PHP globals. Its base URL is set to our ACS URL, so Destination and Recipient are compared with the real endpoint.

- **SP-initiated only.** The AuthnRequest goes by HTTP-Redirect, with RelayState set to the state. `InResponseTo` must equal the flow's request id. IdP-initiated responses (no `InResponseTo`) are refused, because nothing ties them to a browser that asked.
- **Signature.** The response or the assertion must be signed with one of the connection's certificates. Up to two certificates are kept, for rollover. Unsigned responses fail.
- **Audience** must be the SP entity id: `{APP_URL}/api/v1/auth/sso/saml/{connection}`, one per connection.
- **Recipient and Destination** must be the ACS URL: `{APP_URL}/api/v1/auth/sso/saml/{connection}/acs`.
- **Time conditions.** `NotBefore` and `NotOnOrAfter` on Conditions and SubjectConfirmationData are checked with php-saml's 180-second clock skew.
- **Replay.** An assertion id is remembered with `Cache::add` until its `NotOnOrAfter` (at least 10 minutes), and a second use fails with `replayed`.
- **NameID.** The AuthnRequest asks for an `emailAddress` NameID; `persistent` is accepted. A `transient` NameID is new at every sign-in and can name no linked user, so it is refused (`name_id_transient`).
- **Email** comes from the NameID when it is an email address, else the `email` or `emailaddress` claim attribute. **Subject** is the NameID.
- **Metadata.** The IdP's metadata can be imported by URL or by pasted XML.
  - URL fetches are SSRF-guarded: https only, the host must resolve only to public addresses (never private, reserved, carrier-grade NAT `100.64.0.0/10`, NAT64 `64:ff9b::/96` or 6to4 `2002::/16`), pinned to them, no redirects, a 10-second timeout, and the body read as a stream with a running 1 MB cap.
  - The parsed entity id, sign-in URL and certificates are shown and saved like typed ones.
  - A URL is kept. `sso:refresh-metadata` (daily, on one server, tenants from `app_tenants_with_sso_metadata_urls()` through `DueTenants`) reads it again and adds a signing certificate the IdP rotated in as the second slot, next to the current one, so sign-in survives the IdP's rollover. The connection stays enabled and its links stay, because it is the same IdP. Metadata naming another entity id is never followed. These are audited as `core.sso.metadata_refresh` and `_refresh_failed`.

### Linking (never across tenants)

Everything runs in the flow's tenant, under RLS. The checks run in this order:

1. **Verified domain.** The asserted email's domain must be one of the tenant's verified email domains (`domain_not_verified`). A tenant can therefore never assert addresses of a domain it has not proven.
2. **An existing live link** `(connection, subject)`. Its user must be active.
3. **An existing active user** with the same email (citext) and a verified email. A link is created and audited (`auth.sso.link`). If that user already has a link to this connection with another subject, the sign-in is refused (`identity_conflict`). **An Owner is never linked by email** (`owner_link_required`).
4. **An open invitation** for that email. One granting an Owner role is never accepted by SSO (`owner_link_required`). It is accepted as the invitation would be, with every assignment re-checked as the inviter (`Invitations::acceptWithSso`). The user gets an unusable random password: they can set one by password reset unless SSO is enforced.
5. **Just-in-time provisioning**, only when the tenant switched it on (off by default).
   - The new user gets the default role at the default scope. That choice is re-checked as the Owner who set it (`jit_stale` otherwise).
   - Owner roles cannot be the default.
   - The usage limit for users applies (LIM-).
6. **Otherwise** the sign-in is refused (`no_account`).

**Never across tenants.** Emails are unique across the platform, so another tenant's user is never found under this tenant's RLS. JIT provisioning with such an email fails on the unique index and is refused as `no_account`. The tests prove both.

**Owners link themselves.** Whoever controls the IdP could otherwise take over the Owner by creating an account with the Owner's address. An Owner, or any user, links their own account while signed in: Settings, Security, "Link my account" (`POST me/sso/link`). The flow then signs in at the IdP, which must assert that user's own email, of a verified domain; the subject must not be another user's. The link is audited as `auth.sso.link` with `via: self`. A connection test by an Owner reports `owner_link` instead of failing.

**Links can be removed.** A user sees and removes their own links (`GET me/sso`, `POST me/sso/identities/{id}/archive`). An SSO manager sees and removes a user's links (`GET users/{user}/sso-identities`, `POST .../{id}/archive`, also on the user's page). Removing archives the link (TEN-06) and is audited as `auth.sso.unlink`. Live links are unique per subject and per user on a connection; archived ones stay as history.

**Excluded users.**

- Platform staff use their own console and never tenant SSO.
- An assisted tenant's setup account (`is_platform_staff`) is never linked or signed in (ONB-08).
- Deactivated and pending users are refused.

### Lockout, sessions, audit

Completion goes through the same checks as password sign-in:

- a locked account is refused (423 `locked`, AUTH-10);
- a deactivated one gets 403.

The token comes from `issueToken`, so the session list, idle timeout (AUTH-09), new-device alert and the `auth.sign_in` entry apply unchanged. SSO failures with a known tenant are audited as `auth.sso.sign_in_failed` with the reason. They do not count toward the password lockout, because no password was tried.

Audited actions (AUD-01):

- `auth.sso.sign_in` (connection, whether MFA was asserted);
- `auth.sso.sign_in_failed`;
- `auth.sso.link` (`via`: email, invitation, provision or self) and `auth.sso.unlink`;
- `auth.sso.provision`;
- `core.sso.settings_update`;
- `core.sso.connection_create`, `_update`, `_enable`, `_disable`, `_archive` and `_test`. These record settings before and after, and whether the secret changed, never the secret itself;
- `core.sso.domain_add`, `_verify`, `_fail`, `_lost`, `_retry` and `_archive`;
- `core.sso.identities_archive`, `core.sso.metadata_refresh` and `_refresh_failed`.

### Two-factor (decision)

An SSO sign-in skips the app's second factor only when the IdP asserts MFA:

- for OIDC, `amr` contains `mfa`;
- for SAML, the AuthnContextClassRef, or Entra's `authnmethodsreferences` attribute, names a multi-factor context (`http://schemas.microsoft.com/claims/multipleauthn`, `https://refeds.org/profile/mfa`, or the SAML `MobileTwoFactor…` and `TimeSyncToken` classes).

The asserted MFA then also satisfies a role that requires two-factor (AUTH-03), and the token gets full abilities.

Without an MFA assertion, the app's 2FA applies as today:

- a user with 2FA gets the usual challenge (`two_factor_required`), finished by `POST auth/two-factor/challenge`;
- a user whose role requires 2FA but who has none gets an enrol-only token.

The IdP is the tenant's chosen authority. Asking again for a factor it has just checked would only train users to approve prompts.

### Enforced SSO

With `enforce` on, a tenant needs at least one enabled connection and one verified domain; switching it on without them is refused. Then:

- **Password sign-in** is refused (403 `sso_required`) for users of that tenant whose email domain is one of its verified domains. The check runs after the password is checked, so it reveals nothing to someone without the password, and wrong passwords still count toward the lockout.
- **Break-glass.** Users holding the Owner role at tenant scope keep password sign-in and the app's 2FA (RBAC-10). A broken IdP can never lock the business out.
- **OTP sign-up** with an email in a domain verified and enforced by any tenant is refused (422 `sso_required`). Such a person belongs to that organisation and must be invited or provisioned there.
- **Invitations** to someone of a verified domain are accepted by signing in with SSO, not with a password (403 `sso_required`), unless the invitation grants an Owner role.
- **Password reset** still works, because it reveals nothing. The new password is refused at sign-in like the old one.

### Testing the connection

`POST sso/connections/{id}/test` starts a flow marked as a test, bound to the Owner who asked. The callback validates exactly as for a sign-in and resolves which user it would link to, but never signs anyone in. It stores the result on the connection (`tested_at`, `last_test`: ok or the error code, the asserted email, MFA, the user it would link to), audits `core.sso.connection_test`, and sends the browser back to the settings page.

Enabling a connection requires a passing test since its last settings change.

**Repointing (revised decision, after review).** The first version kept an enabled connection enabled whatever was edited. That is kept only for settings that do not name the IdP: the name, the secret and Google's hosted domain clear the test result, but the connection stays enabled.

A change to an **identity-bearing** setting (`client_id`, `directory_id`, `idp_entity_id`, `idp_certificates`) points the connection at another IdP, whose subjects mean other people. In the same transaction:

- every link of the connection is archived (`core.sso.identities_archive`);
- an enabled connection is disabled (`core.sso.connection_disable`, reason `repointed`) until a new test passes.

While SSO is enforced, repointing the last enabled connection is refused (409 `enforce_needs_connection`); turn enforcement off first. The scheduled metadata refresh is not a repoint: it only adds a certificate published by the same entity.

## Consequences

- No tenant can assert identities for a domain it has not proven by DNS, and none can link to another tenant's users. The checks are the verified-domain rule, RLS and the global email uniqueness. The SSO tests and the isolation suite cover them.
- **State lives in the cache.** Flows, tickets and the replay set are kept in the cache store (Redis in production), not in tables. A cache flush only cancels sign-ins in progress. The replay set must outlive assertions, so the cache must not be the `array` store outside tests.
- **SAML signing.** No signed AuthnRequests and no encrypted assertions at first. Both IdPs accept that. The SP has no private key to manage. Adding one later is a settings change.
- **Real IdP registrations are owner inputs.** The product cannot create them, and each tenant registers its own:
  - an Entra app registration (client id, secret, directory id, the redirect URI `{APP_URL}/api/v1/auth/sso/oidc/callback`);
  - a Google OAuth client in the Workspace's Cloud project (same redirect URI);
  - or a SAML app with the entity id and ACS URL shown in settings.
- `firebase/php-jwt`, `phpseclib/phpseclib`, `league/oauth1-client`, `socialiteproviders/manager` and `robrichards/xmlseclibs` arrive as dependencies of the approved packages.
